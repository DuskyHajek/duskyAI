AIB-C01 terms and concepts

Short definitions for the AWS Certified AI Business Strategist exam. Not an AWS product.

Use these with the AIB-C01 practice exam. What the exam covers is defined in the AWS exam guide.

A/B testing

Run the AI process and the current process on similar cases, then compare. That is how a later result can be attributed to the AI rather than to other changes.

Accountable owner

The one business leader answerable for an AI system’s outcomes, risks, and decisions.

AI agent

A system that plans steps, uses business tools, checks the result, and acts or escalates. A chatbot only replies.

AI center of excellence

The team that holds AI standards, reusable assets, and enablement so the company can scale.

AI literacy

The skill to use, question, and govern AI in one’s own role.

Amazon Bedrock

The managed way to build on foundation models, including Knowledge Bases, Guardrails, and agents.

Amazon Quick

The AI workspace for business users: analysis, research, and automation.

Amazon SageMaker AI

Where a team builds, trains, deploys, and monitors its own model when a ready-made one will not fit.

Artificial intelligence

The umbrella term for machines performing tasks that normally need human intelligence. Machine learning, generative AI, and rule-based automation are more specific.

Augmentation

AI recommends or drafts, and a person decides. Automation is when the system acts.

Baseline

The measurement taken before AI, so later change can be credited to the initiative.

Bias

A systematic difference in how the system treats similar people or cases. It can enter at any stage, and bias drift is that unfairness changing over time.

Build-buy-partner

The sourcing choice: build it, buy it, or work with a partner, judged on time, skill, cost, and regulation.

Context window

How much text the model can consider at once: instructions, history, retrieved documents, and the answer.

Drift

The world moves and the model gets worse. Data drift is a change in the inputs. Concept drift is a change in what the right answer is. Model drift is the drop in performance.

EU AI Act

EU law that sorts AI by risk and scales the obligations to that tier.

Explainability

Being able to tell why the system produced an output.

Fine-tuning

Further training of a foundation model on your examples so its behavior changes.

Foundation model

A large model pretrained on broad data and then adapted to many tasks.

Generative AI

Models that create new text, images, audio, or code from a prompt.

Example: summarizing support calls for agents.

Guardrails

Controls on inputs and outputs: blocked topics, sensitive-data filters, and checks that the answer stays on the source.

Hallucination

Fluent output that is false or not supported by the source.

Human-in-the-loop

A person approves each decision before it counts. Human-on-the-loop means the system acts and a person can override it.

Inference

Using a trained model on a new case.

Example: scoring this month’s loan applications with a model trained on past ones.

ISO/IEC 22989

The shared dictionary for AI terms, so teams and vendors mean the same thing.

ISO/IEC 23053

A description of the parts of a machine-learning system and how they fit together. It does not certify the company.

ISO/IEC 42001

The certifiable standard for how an organization manages AI: policy, roles, risk, controls, and improvement. A vendor’s certificate does not certify its customers.

Knowledge Bases

Bedrock’s way of retrieving company documents at question time so answers can be grounded and cited.

KPI

The metric for the outcome the initiative exists to change. KPI means key performance indicator.

Leading and lagging indicators

A leading indicator is an early signal, such as adoption or usage, visible before the business result. A lagging indicator is the result after the project is underway, such as revenue, cost saved, or ROI.

Machine learning

Models that learn patterns from historical data to predict or classify.

Example: predicting which customers will cancel next month.

NIST AI RMF

The US framework for AI risk: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. NIST AI RMF is the AI Risk Management Framework.

Pilot purgatory

Many pilots that never reach production, because the operational pieces a real deployment needs were never built.

Prompt engineering

Writing the instruction, context, constraints, and examples so the output is usable.

Proof of concept

A small, time-boxed test of whether an approach can work at all, before a pilot or a rollout.

Retrieval augmented generation (RAG)

Fetch the relevant company content, then give it to the model with the question.

Risk classification

Sorting use cases by possible harm and applying controls that match the tier.

ROI

Return on investment: benefits minus costs, divided by costs.

Rule-based automation

Fixed logic written by people, with no learning.

Example: routing invoices over a set amount to a manager.

Shadow AI

People using AI tools the organization has not approved.

Structured and unstructured data

Rows and fields, versus documents, email, and images with no fixed schema.

Token

The small piece of text a model reads or writes. Tokens are also how usage and the context window are counted.

Training

Building a model from historical data. Inference is the separate step of using that model on a new case.
