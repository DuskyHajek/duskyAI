AIB-C01 terms and concepts

Short definitions for the AWS Certified AI Business Strategist exam. Not an AWS product.

This AIB-C01 glossary lists the key terms the exam expects you to tell apart. It is not a cheat sheet of questions. Use it with the AIB-C01 practice exam. Last reviewed November 2026 against the AWS exam guide.

70 terms

Concepts

Don’t mix up

The exam often asks which of two or three similar ideas fits the scenario.

Prompt engineering vs retrieval augmented generation vs fine-tuning

Prompt engineering improves the instruction, context, and examples you give the model. Retrieval augmented generation fetches current company content at question time. Fine-tuning retrains the model on your examples so its behavior changes.

On the exam, start with the cheapest change that fixes the problem. Unclear output points to the prompt, missing or weekly-changing facts point to retrieval, and a consistent style or behavior the prompt cannot reach points to fine-tuning.

Artificial intelligence vs machine learning vs generative AI

Artificial intelligence is the umbrella term. Machine learning learns patterns from historical data to predict or classify. Generative AI creates new text, images, audio, or code from a prompt.

On the exam, scoring or predicting from past data is machine learning, creating new content is generative AI, and fixed if-then logic is neither.

Rule-based automation vs AI

Rule-based automation follows fixed logic written by people and never learns. AI handles cases too varied or unstructured to write rules for.

On the exam, if the logic is simple, stable, and must be auditable, rules beat AI. If the inputs are messy or the patterns change, AI earns its cost.

AWS Quick vs Bedrock vs SageMaker AI

AWS Quick, which the exam guide still calls Amazon Quick, is the ready-made workspace for business users. Amazon Bedrock is the managed platform for building a generative AI application on a foundation model, including retrieval and guardrails. Amazon SageMaker AI is where a team builds and trains a custom model when a ready-made one will not fit.

On the exam, business users with no builders point to Quick. A developer building a generative AI app points to Bedrock. A team that needs its own model points to SageMaker AI.

AI agent vs chatbot

A chatbot replies. An AI agent plans steps, uses business tools, checks the result, and acts or escalates.

On the exam, a system that must take actions across tools is an agent. One that only answers questions is an assistant.

Augmentation vs automation

Augmentation keeps a person doing the job, with AI as the draft or recommendation. Automation lets the system carry out the job.

On the exam, a person still producing the outcome is augmentation. Whether each case must be approved is a separate human-in-the-loop question.

KPI vs ROI vs baseline

A KPI is the metric the initiative exists to move. ROI is benefits minus costs, divided by costs. A baseline is the measurement taken before AI, so the later change can be credited to it. Total cost of ownership is the cost side of that ROI, not a third name for the KPI.

On the exam, a number with no before-measurement is not yet a result.

Impact vs feasibility

Business impact is the value if the use case works. Feasibility is whether the data, skills, risk, and time allow it to work.

On the exam, the first use case scores high on both. High impact with weak data is a data project first, not a pilot.

Human-in-the-loop vs human-on-the-loop

Human-in-the-loop means a person approves each decision before it counts. Human-on-the-loop means the system acts and a person can override it.

On the exam, a high-harm or low-confidence case is escalated to a person. A high-harm decision that must not go out unreviewed is human-in-the-loop. A system that acts, with someone able to stop it, is human-on-the-loop.

Explainability vs transparency

Explainability is being able to say why the system produced an output. Transparency is telling people that AI is involved and what it can and cannot do.

On the exam, a regulator asking why a decision was made is explainability. A customer who should know they are talking to AI is transparency.

ISO/IEC 22989 vs 23053 vs 42001

ISO/IEC 22989 is the shared vocabulary. ISO/IEC 23053 describes the parts of a machine-learning system and does not certify the company. ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable management system for how an organization runs AI.

On the exam, a vendor’s 42001 certificate does not certify its customers, and 23053 is not a certificate at all.

Proof of concept vs pilot vs scale

A proof of concept tests whether an approach can work at all. A pilot tests it with real users and real conditions. Scaling builds the ownership, support, and operations that production needs.

On the exam, a successful demo is not production readiness. Many pilots with no owner or operating plan is pilot purgatory.

Accountable owner vs center of excellence

The accountable owner answers for one AI system’s outcomes and risks. The AI center of excellence provides standards, reusable assets, and enablement across the company.

On the exam, who answers for a failure is the owner. Who helps every team do it well is the center of excellence.

A

A/B testing

A/B testing runs the AI process and the current process on similar cases, then compares them. A later result can then be attributed to the AI rather than to other changes.

Accountable owner

An accountable owner is the one business leader answerable for an AI system’s outcomes, risks, and decisions.

AI agent

An AI agent is a system that plans steps, uses business tools, checks the result, and acts or escalates. A chatbot only replies.

AI center of excellence

An AI center of excellence is the team that holds AI standards, reusable assets, and enablement so the company can scale.

AI literacy

AI literacy is the skill to use, question, and govern AI in one’s own role.

AI use-case types

AI use-case types are the common categories a scenario is asking you to recognize: recommendation engines, natural language processing, computer vision, document extraction, and AI for customer operations. Recommendation ranks items for a person, natural language processing works with text or speech, computer vision works with images, and document extraction pulls fields out of forms and contracts.

Amazon Bedrock

Amazon Bedrock is the managed way to build on foundation models, including Knowledge Bases, Guardrails, and agents.

Amazon SageMaker AI

Amazon SageMaker AI is where a team builds, trains, deploys, and monitors its own model when a ready-made one will not fit.

Artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence is the umbrella term for machines performing tasks that normally need human intelligence. Machine learning, generative AI, and rule-based automation are more specific.

Augmentation

Augmentation is when AI recommends or drafts and a person decides.

Automation

Automation is when the system acts on a decision. A person may watch it, but the system does not wait for approval on each case.

AWS Cloud Adoption Framework

The AWS Cloud Adoption Framework is AWS’s plan for scaling adoption across business, people, governance, platform, security, and operations.

AWS Cost Explorer

AWS Cost Explorer is the tool for seeing what you have already spent, and where.

AWS Marketplace

AWS Marketplace is the catalog for buying a partner’s software, data, or model instead of building it. It is one path in a build-buy-partner decision.

AWS Pricing Calculator

The AWS Pricing Calculator estimates what a workload will cost before you buy.

AWS Quick

AWS Quick, which the exam guide still calls Amazon Quick, is the AI workspace for business users: analysis, research, and automation. The two names are the same product.

B

Baseline

A baseline is the measurement taken before AI, so later change can be credited to the initiative.

Bias

Bias is a systematic difference in how the system treats similar people or cases. It can enter at any stage, and bias drift is that unfairness changing over time.

Build-buy-partner

Build-buy-partner is the sourcing choice: build it, buy it, or work with a partner, judged on time, skill, cost, and regulation.

C

Change management

Change management is the work of moving people to a new way of working: communication, incentives, training, and sponsorship. A model in production still fails if the team keeps the old process.

Context window

A context window is how much text the model can consider at once: instructions, history, retrieved documents, and the answer.

D

Data governance

Data governance is who owns the data, who may use it, and how it is kept fit for an AI initiative. Silos and unclear ownership block both quality and permission.

Data quality

Data quality is whether the data is accurate, complete, current, and fit for the decision. A model cannot repair a source the business does not trust.

Drift

Drift is when the world moves and the model gets worse. Data drift is a change in the inputs. Concept drift is a change in what the right answer is. Model drift is the drop in performance.

E

EU AI Act

The EU AI Act is EU law that sorts AI by risk and scales the obligations to that tier. The four tiers are prohibited, high, limited, and minimal.

Executive sponsorship

Executive sponsorship is a named senior leader who gives an AI initiative authority, resources, and air cover. A champion without that backing cannot settle cross-team conflicts.

Explainability

Explainability is being able to tell why the system produced an output.

F

Fairness

Fairness is the requirement that an AI system’s effects are acceptable across groups of people. Bias is one way fairness fails.

Fine-tuning

Fine-tuning is further training of a foundation model on your examples so its behavior changes.

Foundation model

A foundation model is a large model pretrained on broad data and then adapted to many tasks.

G

Generative AI

Generative AI creates new text, images, audio, or code from a prompt.

Example: summarizing support calls for agents.

Guardrails

Guardrails are controls on inputs and outputs: blocked topics, sensitive-data filters, and checks that the answer stays on the source.

H

Hallucination

A hallucination is fluent output that is false or not supported by the source.

Human-in-the-loop

Human-in-the-loop means a person approves each decision before it counts.

Human-on-the-loop

Human-on-the-loop means the system acts and a person watches and can override it.

I

Inference

Inference is using a trained model on a new case.

Example: scoring this month’s loan applications with a model trained on past ones.

ISO/IEC 22989

ISO/IEC 22989 is the shared dictionary for AI terms, so teams and vendors mean the same thing.

ISO/IEC 23053

ISO/IEC 23053 is a description of the parts of a machine-learning system and how they fit together. It does not certify the company.

ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable standard for how an organization manages AI: policy, roles, risk, controls, and improvement. A vendor’s certificate does not certify its customers.

K

Knowledge Bases

Knowledge Bases are Bedrock’s way of retrieving company documents at question time so answers can be grounded and cited.

KPI

A KPI, or key performance indicator, is the metric for the outcome the initiative exists to change.

L

Leading and lagging indicators

A leading indicator is an early signal, such as adoption or usage, visible before the business result. A lagging indicator is the result after the project is underway, such as revenue, cost saved, or ROI.

M

Machine learning

Machine learning is the use of models that learn patterns from historical data to predict or classify.

Example: predicting which customers will cancel next month.

N

NIST AI RMF

The NIST AI RMF is the US framework for AI risk: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. NIST AI RMF means the AI Risk Management Framework.

P

Phased rollout

A phased rollout moves an AI initiative from a proof of concept to a pilot and then to production, widening scope only after each stage holds.

Pilot purgatory

Pilot purgatory is many pilots that never reach production, because the operational pieces a real deployment needs were never built.

Predictive AI

Predictive AI scores or classifies a case from past data.

Pricing models

Pricing models for AWS AI services are consumption-based, instance-based, and seat-based. Consumption-based means you pay for what you use, such as tokens. Instance-based means you pay for capacity that is running. Seat-based means you pay per person, as with a business workspace.

Privacy

Privacy in AI is obtaining, using, and keeping data in a way the people it is about would recognize as legitimate. A useful model is not a reason to collect more than the decision needs.

Prompt engineering

Prompt engineering is writing the instruction, context, constraints, and examples so the output is usable.

Proof of concept

A proof of concept is a small, time-boxed test of whether an approach can work at all, before a pilot or a rollout.

R

Readiness and maturity

Readiness is whether the organization can adopt AI now, across leadership, data, culture, infrastructure, and governance. Maturity is how far along that journey is, from isolated experiments to enterprise-scale use.

Retrieval augmented generation (RAG)

Retrieval augmented generation (RAG) fetches the relevant company content and then gives it to the model with the question.

Responsible AI

Responsible AI is building and using AI so it is fair, transparent, private, safe, and robust, with people accountable for the outcome.

On the exam it shows up as a business tradeoff, not a technical fix.

Risk classification

Risk classification is sorting use cases by possible harm and applying controls that match the tier.

Robustness

Robustness is the system still producing a correct output when the input is unexpected or slightly off.

ROI

ROI, or return on investment, is benefits minus costs, divided by costs.

Rule-based automation

Rule-based automation is fixed logic written by people, with no learning.

Example: routing invoices over a set amount to a manager.

S

Safety

Safety is blocking harmful output and misuse of an AI system. Guardrails are one control, and a person who can stop the action is another.

Savings Plans

Savings Plans are a commitment to a steady amount of AWS usage in exchange for a lower rate. They suit a workload you already know will keep running, not a one-off experiment.

Shadow AI

Shadow AI is people using AI tools the organization has not approved.

Shared responsibility model

The shared responsibility model for AI workloads means AWS secures and operates the service, and the customer is responsible for its data, who may use it, and how the AI is used.

Structured and unstructured data

Structured data is rows and fields. Unstructured data is documents, email, and images with no fixed schema.

T

Token

A token is the small piece of text a model reads or writes. Tokens are also how usage and the context window are counted.

Total cost of ownership

Total cost of ownership is the full cost of an AI initiative over its life, including the service, people, data work, and ongoing operation.

Training

Training is building a model from historical data. Inference is the separate step of using that model on a new case.

Transparency

Transparency is giving people enough information to decide whether and how to engage with an AI system. Explainability is the narrower ability to say why one output happened.

U

Upskilling

Upskilling is teaching people the AI skill their role now needs. AI literacy is that skill, and upskilling is how the organization builds it.

Use-case prioritization

Use-case prioritization is ranking AI initiatives by business value, feasibility, and strategic fit, and then choosing to scale, pause, or stop each one.

W

Well-Architected Responsible AI Lens

The AWS Well-Architected Responsible AI Lens is AWS’s governance guidance for building and running an AI use case responsibly. The exam’s dimensions include fairness, explainability, privacy, safety, transparency, and robustness.
